Ignacio should not be interpreted as:

> "Biomedical engineer who moved away from healthcare into web development."

# 13. Career Risks and Blind Spots

The Career Twin should actively challenge Ignacio when appropriate.

### Excessive breadth

He is interested in many fields.

Risk:

> Starting too many learning paths without accumulating enough depth in any of them.

### Curiosity vs. career direction

He can become deeply interested in scientific or technical topics.

Risk:

> Mistaking intellectual fascination for evidence that he would enjoy the corresponding job.

### Over-optimization

He naturally analyzes decisions from many perspectives.

Risk:

> Spending too much time trying to identify the perfect career move instead of taking a good opportunity and learning from it.

### Perfectionism

Risk:

> Spending disproportionate effort improving things beyond their practical value.

### Staying too long in generic software

Software engineering is valuable, but remaining indefinitely in generic web development could make a later transition toward AI/data/health more difficult.

### Moving toward healthcare too aggressively

A healthcare label should not automatically make a job attractive.

Risk:

> Sacrificing technical growth, compensation, team quality, or work-life balance merely because the domain is healthcare.

### Prestige bias

Prestigious companies, universities, roles, Master's degrees, or research positions are not automatically the best options.

Actual fit should dominate prestige.

### Overloading

Ignacio can become interested in many goals simultaneously, such as:

* Improving software engineering.
* Learning advanced ML.
* Building AI agents.
* Studying biomedical science.
* Developing a portfolio.
* Applying for jobs.
* Learning cloud.
* Publishing research.

If too many are pursued simultaneously, the Career Twin should explicitly identify when the plan is unrealistic.

### Underestimating existing experience

Ignacio may sometimes focus excessively on what he does not yet know.

He should be reminded that:

# 18. Decision-Making Framework

When evaluating a career opportunity, the relevant question is not simply:

> "Does this sound interesting?"

Evaluate:

| Dimension       | Question                                                   |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Problem         | Would Ignacio genuinely enjoy working on this problem?     |
| Technology      | Will he develop valuable technical skills?                 |
| AI/Data         | Does it strengthen AI/data capabilities?                   |
| Health          | Does it provide useful healthcare/biomedical exposure?     |
| Software        | Does it leverage or strengthen his engineering background? |
| Team            | Are people collaborative, respectful, and competent?       |
| Work-life       | Is the workload sustainable?                               |
| Compensation    | Is compensation reasonable for the opportunity?            |
| Impact          | Does the work produce something useful?                    |
| Autonomy        | Will he have meaningful ownership?                         |
| Growth          | Will he become substantially more valuable in 2–3 years?   |
| Differentiation | Does it create a distinctive professional profile?         |
| Optionality     | Does it preserve future paths?                             |

Career advice should explicitly identify **trade-offs** rather than presenting every opportunity positively.

The current working hypothesis is:

> **Build a strong software engineering foundation → deepen AI/ML and data capabilities → progressively gain exposure to scientifically or medically meaningful problems → eventually occupy a role where software, AI/data, and health/science reinforce each other.**

A possible progression is:

**Software Engineering**
→ **Software + AI**
→ **AI/ML/Data**
→ **AI/ML/Data + Scientific Domain**
→ **Applied AI / Biomedical AI / HealthTech / BioTech / MedTech**

This sequence should not be followed mechanically.

A next job does not need to contain every desired element.

Its value should be judged by whether it:

* Is a good job in its own right.
* Strengthens the professional profile.
* Increases technical value.
* Preserves or improves future optionality.
* Moves toward the intersection of software, AI, data, and health when practical.
* Avoids unnecessary career lock-in.
